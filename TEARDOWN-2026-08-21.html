Full teardown · 679 closed trades · 24 Apr – 21 Aug 2026

The agent is flat.
One arm is carrying it.

Every closed trade in the live database, scored on exit return with 4 bps of round-trip slippage charged, then re-run against the actual minute bars to find what a different set of exit rules would have paid. The strategy that works is buried inside a book that is 63% dead weight.

Position size is $5,000 of notional per trade — fixed, not risk-based. Percent figures are the sum of per-trade returns, which is what the dashboard shows; dollars are that percentage applied to $5,000. Every number on this page is net of 4 bps unless it says gross.

The short version

Four months of trading nets $129

All-time the book is +2.59% summed, or +$129 at $5k a trade, over 679 trades and 73 trading days. That is not a strategy result. It is the sum of two dead legacy versions that lost money, one live arm that loses money, and one live arm that makes it.

Answering the sizing question

How much money is actually in each trade

The broker config sizes by fixed notional: sizingMode: "notional", defaultNotional: 5000. Every order is $5,000 of stock regardless of how far away the stop is. Average realised fill notional across the A/B era is $4,912, because market orders round to whole shares.

Signals tracked vs orders actually sent

This is the gap nobody looks at. Of 370 scored trades in the A/B era, only 103 ever became a broker order. The percentage record on the dashboard is the strategy's theoretical record; the dollar record is a different, much smaller book.

Notional deployed per day

Actual submitted orders. Between $10k and $40k of buying power a day, against a $120k daily cap that has never been close to hit.

Peak simultaneous open positions

Counting every tracked signal, the agent has been "in" up to 39 names at once on 13 August. At $5k each that is $195,000 of exposure that the order layer silently refused to take.

Sizing risk, not notional, before real money goes in. A $5,000 position with a 0.4% stop risks $20. The same $5,000 with a 1.5% stop risks $75. Right now the account takes wildly different risk per trade and calls it the same size. risk.riskValue ("1% of account") exists in config and is never read by broker/service.ts. That is the single unglamorous fix that makes every number below trustworthy.
What we could have made

The entries are fine. The exits are leaving 60 points on the floor.

Every one of the 370 A/B trades was re-simulated against its real 1-minute bars from entry to the 14:58 flatten, testing 60 stop / target / trailing combinations. Same entries, same days, same symbols — only the exit rule changes. Slippage charged identically. This is not a backtest of a new idea; it is the same trades exited differently.

The strongest finding

Five symbols make the money. Fifteen burn it.

Rank each watchlist symbol at 09:35 by its own 5-day average daily range plus its own 09:30–09:34 opening range. Two structural inputs, computed before any trade is taken, fitted to nothing. Take only the top five. This finding first appeared over 5 days; it now holds over 18.

Top-5 by volatility rank vs everything else

v11, live exits, net

71 trades in the top-5 bucket returned +40.19% with a −6.07% max drawdown. The 221 trades outside it returned +1.06% with a −23.74% drawdown, and are −18.27% once 4 August is removed. The filter does not just add return, it removes almost all of the drawdown — and drawdown is what caps position size.

Ranked by dollars per unit of work

Five changes, in order

Each figure is the measured difference on the real 370 trades, at $5,000 a position, over the 18 live days. Stacking them is not additive; the combined stack is at the bottom.

  1. Fix 01 — selection

    Only trade the top 5 volatility-ranked symbols

    drawdown −19.96% → −6.07%

    Rank all 20 names at 09:35 on (5-day average range % + opening 5-minute range %) and refuse entries outside the top five. Return barely changes (+41.25% → +40.19%) but 221 losing-side trades disappear and the drawdown falls by two thirds. That is a 3x increase in the position size the same account can carry.

    Build it as a hard gate in the entry path, not an observer. The dayRegime.ts snapshot already computes the inputs at 09:35.

  2. Fix 02 — exits

    Replace v11's fixed 2.5% target with a trailing stop

    +41.25% → +70.11% (+$1,443)

    Stop 1.5%, arm the trail at +1.5%, give back 1.0%. Holds up on every robustness check: +19.18% excluding 4 August (against +3.49% for the live config), +48.34% excluding its own top 3 trades, positive in both halves of the sample, 10 of 17 days green instead of 7. Win rate goes from 43% to 56%.

    The gentler variant, stop 0.8% with the trail armed at +2.0 giving back 1.5, earns slightly less (+65.99%) but has the lowest drawdown of anything tested at −14.64%. If size matters more than total, take that one.

  3. Fix 03 — kill the losing arm

    Widen v10's stop to 1.0% or shut it down

    −15.58% → +4.62% (+$1,010)

    v10's 0.40% stop is inside the noise: 55 of 78 trades stopped, average −0.45%, win rate 15%. The retest entry is not the problem — same entries with a 1.0% stop and a 2.5% target turn the arm from −15.58% to +4.62%. Even then it is only break-even excluding 4 August, and it is negative excluding its own top 3.

    Honest read: the retest arm has not earned its slot. Widen the stop for two more weeks; if it is still under +10% at 40 trades an arm, retire it and run one book.

  4. Fix 04 — sizing

    Size by risk, not by notional

    prerequisite for real money

    Make broker/service.ts read risk.riskValue and solve quantity from (account equity × risk %) ÷ (entry − stop). Today a 0.4% stop and a 1.5% stop both get $5,000, which means the agent is quietly taking 4x the risk on the wide-stop arm. Nothing else on this page can be trusted in dollars until this is true.

    While in there: placeBrokerStopAfterEntry() still passes qty=null, so broker-native stops have never once worked. The only thing protecting a position is the software stop in outcomeTracker, which dies with the process. That is an unacceptable risk with real money in the account.

  5. Fix 05 — close the measurement gap

    Make the dashboard show the ordered book, not the shadow book

    honesty, not return

    Only 103 of 370 A/B signals reached the broker. The 100 ordered trades returned +10.94%; the 270 that never reached the order layer returned +14.73%. The dashboard's headline number is mostly trades that never existed. Add a filter for "reached broker" and default the Analytics page to it, so paper performance and orderable performance stop being the same chart.

Before real money

What would have to be wrong for this to be wrong

  • 18 trading days is not a sample. 71 trades in the recommended stack. One outlier day is 51% of the total. This is a hypothesis with promising evidence, not a proven edge.
  • The live record flatters itself. Stop and target fills are recorded at the exact stop or target price with zero slippage. 4 bps round-trip is charged everywhere on this page, but real market-order fills on a 1-minute stop trigger will be worse than that in fast tape.
  • The counterfactuals check adverse before favourable inside each bar. That is the conservative assumption, but a bar that touched both the stop and the target is scored as a stop, which understates the trailing configs slightly and overstates nothing.
  • Direction is not predictable and was not predicted. The volatility rank says the day will be big, not which way. On 31 July the day signal was in the 96th percentile and v11 lost 7.17%.
  • Dollar P&L on the broker account is −$219 all-time across 304 trades with fills. The percentage record and the dollar record disagree because most signals never became orders. Fix 05 exists so that stops being true.
  • The order path has never been fully exercised. Two orders in the entire history reached FILLED status in broker_orders; native stops have never worked. Arm live money on one symbol at $500 for a week before anything else.
