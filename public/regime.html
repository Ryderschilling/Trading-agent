Observer only. Nothing on this page gates a signal, an order, or a cap. It records what the day looked like at 09:35 ET so the two open questions can be answered with real trades instead of a backtest: does a volatile day pay, and does trading the top-ranked movers beat trading whatever breaks first.
By volatility quintile
Q1 is the most volatile fifth of days, Q5 the quietest. Score is QQQ prior-day range + first five minutes' range + 5-day average range, z-scored.
Over the 116 days studied on 2026-08-04, Q1 produced a big runner on 26% of days and Q5 on 0%. That study measured how far symbols moved, not whether the agent made money. 2026-07-31 scored the 96th percentile and the chase arm lost 7.17%. If the sums below stay flat across quintiles once there are 40+ trades in each, volatility is not worth sizing on and this whole idea is dead.
Top-5 rank vs the rest
Each symbol scored at 09:35 by its own 5-day average range plus its first five minutes' range. Top 5 = the names most likely to move today.
This is the untested one. On 2026-08-03 the agent traded the mega caps while COIN, HOOD, MU, AMD and NVDA ran 4.7% to 7.1%, and lost. On 2026-08-04 it caught PLTR and SMCI and had its best day. Only 8 days of full 20-symbol candle history exist, so nothing here is proven. Give it four to six weeks.
