Backtest & proposal · 601 entries · 73 sessions · 22 Aug 2026

You can't stop it
depending on big days.

You can stop the other fifty days from eating them. Every recorded entry from all four strategy versions, replayed against real minute bars under 1,953 exit configurations, scored out-of-sample. Two changes survive every honesty test. Nothing here has been deployed.

Sample is the 601 chase entries (v6 + v9 + v11), 24 Apr → 21 Aug 2026, plus 78 v10 retest entries tracked separately. v6 and v9 traded chase by accident — the retest-tolerance bug — so their entries are mechanically the same thing v11 does on purpose. All 679 are replayable: every trade has minute bars from its entry to the close.

What this strategy actually is

Before any parameter talk. Take the best configuration found anywhere in this study, apply the tightest sensible symbol filter, and remove the five best days out of fifty-five. What's left is +3.22%.

Change one

Drop the fixed target. Trail instead.

A 2.5% target caps the only trades that pay for everything else. Over 601 entries and five months, every trailing variant beats every fixed-target variant, and the win is not a single lucky cell in the grid.

Stop width down the side, trail arming point across the top (give-back is always half the arm). An overfit winner is a lone bright cell surrounded by dark ones. This is a ridge: the whole arm +2.0 column wins, and stops from 0.7% to 1.0% form the crest.

The drawdown grid tells you where the crest ends. Stops at 1.25% and wider hold their return but drawdown blows out from −24% to −50%, which is the same thing as saying you'd have to trade a third of the size.

The out-of-sample test that matters

Fit the parameters on one half of the history, then score that frozen configuration on the half it has never seen. Run it in both directions, and pick by two different criteria: raw total, and total divided by max drawdown.

The single most useful line on this page. Fit on July–August and rank by raw total, and you get a 1.5% stop with a wide trail: +65.90% in-sample, −1.89% out-of-sample. Rank the same data by return-per-unit-of-drawdown instead and you get 0.80% stop, no target, trail arming at +2.0% giving back 1.0%: +56.81% in-sample and +35.79% out-of-sample at only −14.6% drawdown. Same data, same search, different scoring rule — one config generalises and one doesn't. Never rank a backtest by total again.

Rolling walk-forward, no filter

re-fit every month on prior data only

Choose the best configuration using only what was knowable at the start of each month, then trade it blind for that month. 525 out-of-sample trades, +53.37%, −23.50% drawdown. Positive, but two of four months are negative and the parameters wander — which is the argument for freezing one robust configuration rather than re-optimising.

Change two

Only trade the day's most volatile names

At 09:35 rank every watchlist symbol by its own 5-day average daily range plus its own opening five-minute range, and express that as a percentile of the day's universe rather than a fixed top-N — the watchlist was 11 names in spring and is 20 now. Then refuse everything below the cut.

Hard gate versus size tilt

The alternative to refusing the low-ranked names is trading them small. Tilting has a marginally better return-per-drawdown, but it triples the absolute drawdown, keeps 450 junk trades in the book, and needs risk-based sizing to exist first. Take the gate.

Change three

Size by risk, and know the legal ceiling

With a fixed $5,000 notional, a 0.8% stop risks $40 and a 1.5% stop risks $75. Position size should fall out of the stop, not sit beside it: quantity = (equity × risk %) ÷ (entry − stop).

Every row is the same 109 trades. Only the money management changes. 0.5% of equity per trade is the setting I would use: 36.5% over four months against an 8.3% peak-to-trough, which is a drawdown you can actually sit through.

The constraint no backtest will tell you: pattern day trader rules. This strategy takes two to four day trades per session. In a US margin account under $25,000 of equity you are limited to three day trades in any rolling five business days, and breaking it freezes the account for 90 days. A cash account settles T+1, which makes two trades a day impossible. There is no version of this that runs daily on real money below $25,000. That is the actual gate on going live, not the strategy.
And the gross exposure it implies. Risking 0.5% of equity behind a 0.8% stop means a position worth 62.5% of the account. Median day is one trade, but the 90th percentile day is four. Without a gross cap that is 2.5x equity — margin, and a bad correlated morning takes a chunk out of you. Cap gross exposure at 1.0x equity and let later signals size down into what's left.
Weighed and discarded

Things I tested that I would not build

no

Daily loss limit

Stopping the day after a 1.0% account loss changes the result from $34,136 to $34,641 and does not move drawdown at all. At 0.5% it costs you 19 points. There are only ten days in 55 where it would ever fire. It is a comfort feature that buys nothing measurable, and it risks cutting a day short right before the burst that pays for the month.

no

Per-symbol daily cooldown

Proposed after week one of the A/B when v11's re-entries looked bad. Over the full sample, re-entries into top-ranked names are the best trades in the book. The bad re-entries were all in low-ranked names, and the volatility gate removes them for a reason that has a mechanism. Adding a cooldown on top would remove the good ones too.

no

Hold past the 14:58 flatten

Holding to 15:58 instead scores +58.13% against +66.69% for the same stop, and goes negative once the best day is removed. The extra hour is noise. Separately, worth knowing: 14:58 is an hour before the actual close, so the flatten time looks like it was written against a 15:00 close. It happens to be the right answer anyway.

later

Breakeven stop after +0.75%

Cuts drawdown from −24.1% to −20.3% and total from +92.6% to +65.4%. It is a fair trade if drawdown is binding, but the volatility gate buys more drawdown reduction for less return, so do that first and re-test this after.

later

Size tilt instead of a hard gate

Better return-per-drawdown on paper. Needs risk-based sizing to exist, triples absolute drawdown, and keeps 450 marginal trades in the book. Revisit once the gate has run live for a month.

keep

The four-trades-per-day cap

Already covered in the teardown: over 18 live days it blocked 44 signals that would have lost 24.9%. Once the volatility gate is in, the cap will rarely bind anyway — the gate produces a median of one trade a day and four at the 90th percentile.

The second question

Can you backtest this reliably? Not today.

There are two backtesters in the repo and neither one tests what the agent does.

What is broken right now

  • The /backtest page uses a different strategy. src/sim/backtestEngine.ts sets the stop to the entry candle's extreme and the target to twice that risk. Production uses a fixed percentage stop and a fixed percentage target from the ruleset. Every backtest run stored in the database — all of them — measured a strategy the agent has never traded.
  • It runs on 5-minute bars by default. Production evaluates 1-minute bars. Different bar size means different break detection, different stop triggers, different everything.
  • It has none of the live gates. No session direction lock, no SPY 1.5% move gate, no reversal gate, no daily notional cap, no MFE gate, no time exit, no EOD flatten. Those gates suppressed roughly two thirds of all signals in the live era.
  • The replay harness is the honest one, and it is scenario-file based. src/replay/harness.ts runs the real engine, but one captured day at a time, and the last saved reports are from May and June.
  • Nothing has ever been scored out of sample. No holdout, no walk-forward, no plateau check. Which is how a 5-day sample produced a "clamping trades per day is negative EV" conclusion that 18 days reversed.

What to build — three tiers, in this order

Tier 1

Exit lab

Exactly what produced this page. Load recorded entries, replay exits against real 1-minute bars, sweep thousands of configurations in seconds. Answers every stop, target, trail, breakeven and time-exit question. Cannot answer entry questions. About 200 lines, no engine changes, zero risk to production. Verified against the live record at r = 0.983.

scripts/exit-lab.ts  --entries all|v11|v10  --sweep stop,target,trail,be
                     --filter rankPct<0.15  --score return/dd
                     --holdout 2026-07-01  --walkforward month
Tier 2

One exit engine, two callers

Pull the exit logic out of outcomeTracker into a pure function that takes a config, an entry and a bar stream, and returns an exit. Have the live tracker and the exit lab both call it. This is the only structural change that makes a backtest number and a live number mean the same thing. It also gives the trailing stop one implementation instead of two.

Tier 3

Full signal replay with the real gates

Drive src/replay/harness.ts over every historical session instead of one captured day, with the live gate stack wired in. Only this can answer entry questions: what a different retest tolerance does, whether raising the per-symbol daily cap from three to five helps, what happens if the session direction lock is removed. Today those signals do not exist in the database because they were never generated, so no amount of analysis on recorded trades will reach them.

The scoring rules that make it trustworthy

The rig is the easy part. This is the part that stops you fooling yourself, and it is worth hard-coding into the tool so a run cannot report a winner without them.

Rank by
Total return ÷ max drawdown. Never by total. Demonstrated above: on the same data, ranking by total produced a config that lost money out of sample and ranking by ratio produced one that made 35.79%.
Plateau
The chosen cell's immediate neighbours in the parameter grid must average within 20% of it. A winner surrounded by losers is a coincidence.
Consistency
At least 60% of calendar months positive, and still positive with the best three days removed.
Concentration
Report best-day share of total on every run. Above 0.60 means you are looking at one day, not a strategy.
Holdout
Every result reported both in-sample and on a date-split holdout, in both directions. A config that only works forward is a config that only works in one regime.
Costs
4 bps round trip charged on every trade, always, and a note that real stop fills will be worse than the recorded ones because the database logs stop exits at the exact stop price.
Minimum
No configuration adopted on fewer than 100 trades or fewer than 3 calendar months.
If it were my money

The order I would do it in

Nothing here changes a live setting before the thing that measures it exists.

Step 1

Build the exit lab and freeze a baseline

Commit it, run it against today's live config, save the output as the baseline. Every future change gets compared to that file. No live change. Half a day.

Step 2

Ship the volatility rank as an observer

The dayRegime.ts snapshot already computes both inputs at 09:35. Write the percentile onto every alert and every outcome row. Change nothing else. Run it a week, then confirm the live ranks match what this study computed. Only after that does it become a gate.

Step 3

Change the exits on v11 only

Stop 0.80%, no fixed target, trail arms at +2.0% and gives back 1.0%. Leave v10 alone so you keep a control. Two weeks.

Step 4

Turn the gate on, and repoint v10

Gate at the top 20% of the day's universe — between the 15% and 25% cells, both of which are on the plateau. At the same time give v10 the identical exits and gate, so the only remaining difference between the arms is retest versus chase. That is the A/B you thought you were running in July.

Step 5

Risk sizing and a stop that survives a crash

Make broker/service.ts read risk.riskValue, 0.5% of equity, gross capped at 1.0x. Fix placeBrokerStopAfterEntry() passing qty=null so a broker-native stop actually exists. Right now the only thing protecting an open position is a software stop that dies with the process, and that process has already been found dead once.

Step 6

Then, and only then, money

Four more weeks of paper on the new configuration first, which gets you to roughly 40 gated trades — the minimum this study's own rules would accept. Then arm live with $500 positions on one arm for two weeks and reconcile every fill against what the tracker thought happened. Scale only after the fills match.

The parts I am least sure about

What would have to be true for this to be wrong

  • One regime. April to August 2026 is a single market environment. Nothing here has seen a sustained downtrend or a volatility crush. A strategy that needs symbols moving 3% a day stops existing in a quiet tape, and the volatility gate will simply produce zero trades — which is the correct behaviour but will feel like the agent is broken.
  • The v6 and v9 entries are a proxy, not the same thing. They are chase entries, but they came from a bug, on a smaller watchlist, through a different gate stack. They quadruple the sample and they are the reason the exit finding is believable. They are not proof that v11 would have produced identical trades in April.
  • Intrabar order is unknowable from minute bars. When a bar touches both the trailing stop and a new high, I score it as the stop. That is conservative for the trailing configs and generous to nothing.
  • Slippage is a flat 4 bps. A market order triggered off a 1-minute stop in a name that is moving 3% will do worse than that, and the recorded database logs stop exits at exactly the stop price. The real number is somewhere between this page and worse.
  • 109 trades in the recommended configuration. Five months, but only 109 trades and 55 active days. The plateau test and the reverse holdout are what make me willing to say it out loud; neither is a substitute for forward evidence.
  • And the scale question. At 0.5% risk this returns roughly 9% a month on the account in the sample period. On $25,000 that is about $2,250 a month before tax. It is a real return and it is not a path to $10,000 a month, let alone $100,000 — that would need roughly $1.1M at the same rate. This is a capital compounder, not an income business. Fund it from something else and let it run.
